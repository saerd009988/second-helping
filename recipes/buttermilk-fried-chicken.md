steps:
  - "Whisk 2 tablespoons of the salt and the hot sauce into the buttermilk until the salt dissolves. Submerge the chicken, cover, and refrigerate at least 8 hours and up to 24. The salt brines the meat and the buttermilk's acid tenderizes the surface — this is the difference between juicy and merely cooked."
  - "Whisk the flour, cornstarch, remaining tablespoon of salt, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, black pepper and cayenne in a wide, shallow dish. The cornstarch keeps the crust crisp instead of bready."
  - "Drizzle 4 tablespoons of the brine into the flour and rub it through with your fingers until the mixture is pebbly with small clumps. Those clumps become the craggy bits."
  - "Lift a piece of chicken from the brine, let it drip for 3 seconds, and press it firmly into the flour, packing the coating on from every angle. Set it on a wire rack. Coat everything, then rest 20 minutes until the flour looks pasted and damp rather than dusty — dry coating slides off in the oil."
  - "Pour oil 2 inches deep into a heavy Dutch oven and heat to 350°F over medium-high, tracking it with a clip-on thermometer. Frying without a thermometer is guesswork you will taste."
  - "Lower in 3 or 4 pieces skin side down, never crowding. The oil will drop to about 300°F; adjust the burner to hold 300 to 325°F for the whole fry. Below that the crust absorbs oil; above it the crust burns before the thigh bone is done."
  - "Fry 8 to 10 minutes per side, turning once with tongs. Dark meat needs 165°F at the bone, breasts 160°F — check with an instant-read at the thickest point, avoiding the bone itself."
  - "Drain on a wire rack set over a sheet pan, never on paper towels, and salt lightly the instant it comes out of the oil. A rack keeps air circulating under the crust; paper traps steam and softens it."
  - "Let the oil return to 350°F between batches and skim out loose crumbs, which burn and turn the next batch bitter. Rest the chicken 10 minutes before serving."

## Tips

**Brine long, dry briefly.** Eight hours is the minimum for the salt to reach the middle of a thigh. Twenty-four is better. Past that, the acid starts to make the surface mushy.

**Hold 300 to 325°F, not 350°F.** 350°F is the starting temperature; the chicken pulls it down, and that lower range is where a bone-in piece cooks through before its crust goes too dark.

**Two racks, always.** One for the coated raw chicken to rest on, one for the fried chicken to drain on. Coated chicken that sits in a puddle fries unevenly.

## Variations

- **Nashville hot:** Whisk 1/2 cup of the frying oil with 2 tablespoons cayenne, 1 tablespoon brown sugar, 1 teaspoon garlic powder and a pinch of salt. Brush over the hot chicken and serve on white bread with pickles.
- **Honey butter:** Melt 4 tablespoons butter with 3 tablespoons honey and a pinch of flaky salt; drizzle at the table.
- **Boneless tenders:** Same brine and dredge, but fry at 350°F for 4 to 5 minutes total.
