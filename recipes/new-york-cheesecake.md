steps:
  - "Heat the oven to 325°F. Wrap the outside of a 9-inch springform pan in two layers of heavy-duty foil, pressing it flat up the sides so no water can seep into the seam."
  - "Stir the graham crumbs, 2 tablespoons sugar and melted butter until the mixture looks like wet sand. Press it firmly into the pan bottom and 1 inch up the sides using the flat base of a measuring cup. Bake 10 minutes and cool while you mix the filling."
  - "Beat the room-temperature cream cheese alone on medium-low for 3 minutes, scraping the bowl twice, until completely smooth with no lumps. Lumps present now will still be there after baking — cold cream cheese never fully smooths out."
  - "Add the 1.5 cups sugar and beat 2 minutes more, then the sour cream, vanilla, lemon juice and salt, mixing just to combine. Keep the mixer on low from here: air beaten into the batter expands in the oven and then collapses, and that is what tears the surface."
  - "Add the eggs and yolk one at a time on the lowest speed, mixing only until each disappears, about 15 seconds each. Scrape the bowl bottom with a spatula and fold by hand a few times to catch unmixed batter."
  - "Pour the batter over the cooled crust and rap the pan firmly on the counter 5 or 6 times to release trapped bubbles. Set it in a roasting pan and pour boiling water into the outer pan to come 1 inch up the sides."
  - "Bake 85 to 95 minutes. It is done when the outer 2 inches look set and matte and the center 3 inches still wobbles as a single unit when you nudge the pan — like gelatin, not like liquid. The center reads 150°F. A cheesecake baked until the middle is firm is already overbaked."
  - "Turn the oven off, prop the door open with a wooden spoon and leave the cheesecake in the cooling oven for 1 hour. Sudden temperature drops contract the surface faster than the interior, which is the other cause of cracks."
  - "Lift the pan out of the water bath, remove the foil and run a thin knife around the inside edge to free the cake from the sides. Cool to room temperature on a rack, about 2 hours."
  - "Refrigerate uncovered at least 8 hours, ideally overnight, before unlatching the ring. The texture is not finished until it is fully cold. Slice with a hot, dry knife, wiping it clean between cuts."

## Tips

**Room temperature is not optional.** Cream cheese, eggs and sour cream all at 68 to 70°F emulsify into a smooth batter. One cold ingredient reintroduces lumps at the last moment.

**The water bath does two things.** It caps the outside temperature near 212°F and keeps the oven humid so the surface stays flexible. Skip it and you get a browned, domed, cracked cake.

**A crack is cosmetic.** If one opens up anyway, the cake still tastes right — cover it with sour cream topping or fruit and move on.

## Variations

- **Sour cream top:** Whisk 1.5 cups sour cream with 3 tablespoons sugar and 1 teaspoon vanilla, spread over the just-baked cake and return it to a 325°F oven for 8 minutes.
- **Strawberry:** Simmer 1 pound of hulled strawberries with 1/3 cup sugar and 1 tablespoon lemon juice for 10 minutes and cool before spooning over slices.
- **Espresso:** Dissolve 2 tablespoons of instant espresso powder in the vanilla and add a layer of chocolate cookie crumbs to the crust.
